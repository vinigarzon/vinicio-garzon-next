Life in ten lines

1908Born near Paris, wealthy textile family ("Cartier-Bresson thread"). 1926Studies painting with the Cubist André Lhote: geometry, golden section. Sits with Breton's Surrealists. 1931Sees Munkácsi's Three Boys at Lake Tanganyika: "photography can fix eternity in a moment". 1932Buys a Leica. Early masterpieces: Gare Saint-Lazare, Hyères, Brussels. 1933–35Solo show at Julien Levy Gallery, New York; exhibits with Álvarez Bravo in Mexico City. 1936–39Assistant to film director Jean Renoir. 1940–43Prisoner of war, 35 months; escapes on the third attempt. 1947MoMA "posthumous" show (they thought he was dead); co-founds Magnum Photos with Capa, Chim, Rodger, Vandivert. 1948–54Gandhi's last hours & funeral; China, fall of the Kuomintang; 1952 The Decisive Moment (cover by Matisse); 1954 first Western photographer in the USSR after Stalin. 1970s–2004Returns to drawing. Fondation HCB opens 2003. Dies in Provence, aged 95.

The six rules (techniques)

  1. One Leica, one 50 mm lens: the eye's own angle of view; chrome covered with black tape.
  2. No flash: "like coming to a concert with a pistol in your hand". Available light only.
  3. No cropping: the black film border proves the full frame. Compose in the camera.
  4. No darkroom: a lab printed his work; the picture ends when the shutter closes.
  5. Black & white: light, line, shape, texture, tonal contrast.
  6. Geometry first: find the structure, then wait for life to enter it.

Three Cartier-Bressons (Chéroux, 2014)

1926–35 the Surrealist · 1936–46 the engaged witness · 1947–70s the Magnum eye, then the contemplative.

Vocabulary to listen for

Decisive moment: meaning + geometry in the same instant.

Framing: what the edges include; a "frame within a frame".

Shutter speed: fast = frozen motion (the jump); slow = blur (the cyclist).

Depth of field: how much is sharp, front to back.

Exposure: how light or dark; available light only.

Balance / symmetry: visual weight; a reflection doubles it.

Line & rhythm: ladders, rails, hoops; repeated shapes.

Form / shape / silhouette: the jumping man as a black cut-out.

Texture: plaster, rubble, feathers, wool.

Tonal contrast: deep blacks against bright whites.

Proportion: small figures in big space.

Golden section: the painter's ratio he learned from Lhote.

Artist statement · The Decisive Moment, 1952

"To me, photography is the simultaneous recognition, in a fraction of a second, of the significance of an event as well as of a precise organization of forms which give that event its proper expression."

Epigraph (Cardinal de Retz, 1717): "There is nothing in this world that does not have a decisive moment."

The decisive part of Cartier-Bresson's process takes place not in the mechanism in his hand but in the vision in his head.Lincoln Kirstein · MoMA, 1947
